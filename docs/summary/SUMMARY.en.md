# Proof of One — summary

**Polls you can trust: one person, one voice, counted in the open.**

*Design stage — not audited. This is the source text for translations (CC BY-SA 4.0).*

## The problem
Online polls and petitions can no longer be trusted. Computer programs can pretend to be thousands of people. One person can answer many times. And nobody can check whether a published result is the real count.

## What Proof of One does
It is an open system for polls in which:
- **Every participant is a real person**, checked once with the chip in their passport or ID card and their face, using an ordinary phone.
- **Each person takes part only once** in each poll.
- **Your answer stays private.** No one — not even the people who run the system — can see how you answered.
- **You can check your own vote.** Your phone confirms that your answer was recorded and counted.
- **Anyone can recount.** Every answer is stored, locked, on a public ledger, so anyone can check the totals.

## How it works
1. **Join once.** Your phone reads your document chip and checks your face. No personal data goes on the public ledger.
2. **Answer.** Your phone locks your answer with encryption and adds a mathematical proof that it comes from a real, eligible member.
3. **Count.** The locked answers are added up while still locked. Only the totals are unlocked, with proofs that anyone can check.

## Who chooses the questions
**The participants.** Each month, people propose topics for their own country and for a global track, then choose by vote which polls are run. Taking part is free.

## What it is — and is not
- **Open source and non-commercial.** No token, nothing for sale, no investors.
- **Neutral.** Open to everyone; it takes no political side.
- **Built in the open** by a pseudonymous founder, Nemo Unus, with a team of openly disclosed AI agents. Independent human experts are invited to check everything.

## Honest limits
- **Not yet audited.** It is not ready for use by real participants.
- An authority that issues passports could create false identities. The system makes this costly and visible, but cannot fully prevent it.
