# Second-model review of whitepaper v1.3 (against v1.2, the Decision Log #54–#71, consent v0.5, the app reviews and the code)

*Review by a second AI model. Not an independent human review; it does not count towards Gate 0. Read-only: no file other than this report was changed. Checked: the full v1.2-to-v1.3 diff, then every place in v1.3 that the diff did not touch but that the changes affect (grep for receipt, PIN, duress, enclave, pilot, Greek names, country and founder details). Code checked: `Profiles.kt`, `Backup.kt`, `EnrolActivity.kt`, `FaceCheckActivity.kt`, `VoteActivity.kt`, `member.rs`, `enrol-service/src/service.js`.*

**Result.** 5 major and 12 minor findings. What v1.3 changed is mostly accurate: the password rules, the Argon2id settings (64 MiB, 3 passes), the two encryption layers, the wait after wrong passwords, the five-minute close, the 512-number face template that stays out of the backup, the write-once backup at the service, the member-number derivation, and the three review rounds all match the code. The main problems are in text that v1.3 did **not** touch and that now contradicts the pilot or the new sections, and in two new claims (receipts, "volunteers are told all of this") that the code or the consent text does not support.

## Major findings

**1. Receipts and the receipt window are described as built; the app has neither.**
- Sections: 6.8 (password bullet: "the member's file (… receipts)"; bullet "Receipts are kept in each member's own encrypted file"), 6.11 (row "Content of an answer: own answer, during the receipt window"), 9.2 ("show my recorded answer": "The phone keeps the random values r_j as a private receipt … 30 days, 7 days, or none"), 9.3 ("Receipt window", "No history"), 12.2 (rows "Seized phone reveals past answers", "Coercion"; and the shared-phone row, "receipts encrypted per member"), 17 items 8 and 11, the glossary.
- Problem. There is no receipt store in the Android code (no match for "receipt" anywhere in the app). `VoteActivity.kt` (header, point 2) finds the recorded answer from the contract's ballot hash and re-derives the ballot's random values from the member's secret, the poll and a revision number. So there is no window of 30 days, 7 days or none: for as long as the member's file exists, whoever opens the member (password, or a phone watched while the password is typed) can see the answer to every past poll. Decision #65 records exactly this ("a member's secret opens all that member's past answers (to be stated in the threat model)"). v1.3 also writes into 6.8 that the member's file holds "receipts" and that receipts are encrypted per member, which is wrong for the same reason.
- Why it matters. The threat-model row says a seized phone reveals only "an answer in a poll still inside its receipt window". For the pilot that is untrue and understates the risk to volunteers. It is also an overclaim of a protection the pilot lacks.
- Suggested text.
  - 6.8, password bullet: "…the member's file (identity secret, credential, attributes, face template) is encrypted…"
  - 6.8, replace the Receipts bullet: "**Answers.** The app keeps no separate receipts. It finds a member's recorded answer again from the public record, because the random values of each ballot are derived from the member's secret (Decision Log #62, #65). Anyone who can open the member can therefore see all of that member's past answers. The receipt window of 9.2 is not built."
  - 9.3, first bullet: "**Receipt window (design; not built in the pilot).** The design lets the member choose how long the phone can show their recorded answer: 30 days after the close, 7 days, or none. In the pilot there is no such window: the answer to every poll the member took part in can be shown for as long as the member can be opened. Delete the 'No history' bullet's reference to 'active receipt windows' or mark it the same way."
  - 12.2 row 'Seized phone': residual risk "Answers to all past polls of a member whose password is known or watched; fact of participation". Item 17.8 and 17.11: replace "after the receipt window" with "in the pilot, at any time".
  - Add a new item 17.29: "**Past answers in the pilot.** A member's secret re-opens all of that member's past answers; the receipt window is a design goal only (6.8, 9.3)."

**2. Sections 6.3, 6.11, 7.3, 7.4 and 9.3 still describe the design as if it were the pilot.**
- Problem. v1.3 changed step 1 of 6.3 only. The other steps still say: a platform attestation is added (step 4; the app has no attestation code); the proof, "the chip photo and the live face frames" go over an anonymising network to an enclave (step 5; the pilot sends no face, no photo and uses no anonymising network); the enclave "stores only the fingerprint and the protected template" (step 7; the pilot service stores no template); "hours later … identities are added only in large batches" (step 8; the pilot registers 1–10 minutes later and each leaf enters the tree at once, per #62, #63). 6.11 (face template row: "stored encrypted; compared only in the enclave"; "time of each registration batch") and 9.3 ("**Anonymous network access.** All connections go through an anonymising network"; "**Verifying the app.** Builds are reproducible and the signing-key fingerprint is published in several independent places") are also written in the present tense. 7.4 item 6 still says the fingerprint input is "not yet implemented" and item 5 says the photo is "sent to the enclave"; both are false for the pilot (Decision #58, line 36 of this paper). 7.3's code block says `K_dev = P-256 key pair` and "stored as Enc_K_dev(s); backup end-to-end encrypted"; the app uses an AES-256 Keystore key per member plus a password-derived key (6.8).
- Why it matters. A volunteer or reviewer who reads 6.3 will believe faces go to an enclave over an anonymising network. That is the opposite of what the pilot does, and it contradicts section 1 and the consent text.
- Suggested text. Put a boxed note at the start of 6.3: "**In the pilot** (Decision Log #57, #58, #62, #63) the steps differ from this design: the live face is matched to the chip photo on the phone and nothing about the face is sent; there is no enclave, no attestation and no anonymising network; the service sees the proof's public outputs (date of birth, sex, nationality, expiry month) and reserves two keyed fingerprints; the phone registers on the ledger 1–10 minutes after enrolment, and each registration enters the tree at once. Section 17, item 27 lists the rest." Then mark 6.11's face-template row "Pilot: not sent; the service keeps none". In 9.3 add "(design; not in the pilot)" to "Anonymous network access" and "Verifying the app", and say how the pilot differs: "one fixed signing key; the volunteer checks its fingerprint against the one the organiser publishes". In 7.4 item 6 write "implemented for the pilot (Decision Log #58); the shared key and π_unblind are not (17.25)". In 7.3 write `K_dev = AES-256 key in secure hardware, one per member (the design uses P-256)` and "stored under a password-derived key and under K_dev (6.8); backup under password and backup seed".

**3. Section 6.7's stage table says the pilot compares face templates in an enclave.**
- Section: 6.7, first row of the stage table: "Test network and low-risk pilot (Phases 0–2) | Encrypted templates compared inside a hardware-isolated enclave".
- Problem. The pilot has no enclave, no template at the service and no face search (Decision #57 point 3; 17.27). The row says the opposite and sits next to the new text.
- Suggested text. "Test network (Phases 0–2) | Encrypted templates compared inside a hardware-isolated enclave | …" and add a row: "**The pilot with volunteers (Phase 1p)** | None. The face is matched to the chip photo on the phone only; no template reaches the service; duplicates are found by document fingerprint only | A person with two documents from different issuers could enrol twice; the single operator holds the OPRF key (17.27)".

**4. Item 17.27 and the §14 pilot bullet do not say everything the service sees and keeps, and 17.27 says volunteers are told "all of this"; the consent does not tell them all of it.**
- Code (`service.js`): at enrolment the service receives, in the clear, the proof's public outputs: date of birth, sex, nationality code and expiry month. It stores person key, document key, issuing state, path, enrolment time and the encrypted backup. Consent §10 does list these. The whitepaper says in §14 only that the service "keeps only keyed document fingerprints and an encrypted backup", and in 1 only "keyed document fingerprints".
- Consent v0.5 does not say: that the cloud is a commercial one and there is no enclave; that a person with two documents from different issuers could enrol twice (there is no face search); that each registration enters the tree at once (it says registration takes place 1–10 minutes after enrolment, and that the organiser "could guess" which code is yours, which is close but not the same); that there is no revocation path, so a stolen phone's identity cannot be revoked during the pilot. So "Volunteers are told all of this (consent v0.5)" is not accurate as written.
- Why it matters. This is the one sentence that tells a reader the pilot's deviations are disclosed to the people who bear them.
- Suggested text. 17.27, last sentence: "Volunteers are told the first, fourth and fifth of these in the consent text (v0.5); the others are stated here and are to be added to the consent text before the next version." (Or, better, add them to consent v0.6 and keep the original sentence.) §14 pilot bullet: "…the enrolment service sees, at enrolment, the date of birth, sex, nationality and expiry month proved by the passport and keeps, until the pilot ends, two keyed document fingerprints, the issuing state, the time of enrolment and an encrypted backup it cannot open without the person's document data and password." Also see finding 11 on "cannot open".

**5. Section 1 says the risk is the founder's; it is the volunteers' risk.**
- Sections: 1 ("That risk is the founder's decision and is stated here so that no one is misled"), 15 row 1p ("The founder accepted the risk of running it before independent review"), and the consent summary.
- Problem. The founder made the decision, but the people exposed to a privacy or security flaw are the volunteers, who are real people with real passports, and many may be in countries that restrict the internet (§14 mentions VPNs). Writing that the risk is the founder's reads as if the founder alone carries it. This is the headline paragraph that is meant to correct earlier overclaiming, so it must not itself understate who is exposed.
- Suggested text. §1: "The founder decided this, and the volunteers carry the risk. The consent text tells each volunteer plainly that the code has not been independently reviewed and what can go wrong, and each can refuse or stop at any time." Row 1p: "The founder decided to run it before independent review; the volunteers accept the risk after reading the consent text."

## Minor findings

**6. Sections 1, 13.1 and 15: "Beyond this pilot, nothing will be used with real participants before a public bug bounty and an independent audit" contradicts the roadmap.**
Phase 1b (volunteers on a public test network, with the bounty running) comes before the independent audit (its gate), and 13.1 says human review comes "before the first public pilot with real participants", while Gate 0 is human review of the specifications and code. Suggested text for §1: "Beyond this pilot, nothing will be used with real participants before three independent human reviews of the design and code (Gate 0); use outside low-risk settings waits for a public bug bounty and an independent audit (Sections 14 and 15)." Same fix in the conclusion (18): "beyond it, the rule stands" should say which rule.

**7. Section 6.8, member number example does not match the code.**
The paper shows `ONE-7Q4KM-9M2XH-8T3VA-5RZ0P-K` (five groups of five). `member.rs` and `fingerprint.js` build `ONE-` plus four groups of four plus one check character, and the consent shows `ONE-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-X`. Suggested text: `ONE-7Q4K-9M2X-8T3V-5RZ0-K` ("about 80 bits and a check character"). Also: the paper says the number is the same "after renewing the passport"; that holds only on the document paths where x does not change with the person's name (6.12, 7.5). Add "where the person key does not change (7.5)". Also, consent §5 says "the government that issued your passport could compute the same number"; the paper (correctly, per Decision #59) says it also needs the service's OPRF key. One of the two should be changed, preferably the consent: "…could compute it if it also held the service's key".

**8. Section 6.8, "cannot open the file without that phone's hardware" is stronger than the reviews allow.**
Decision #70 point 5 and the v0.16 review leave to a human expert whether the Keystore key is bound to an unlocked phone and how the weakest phones behave with 64 MiB Argon2; not every phone has hardware-backed keys. Suggested text: "…so that a copy of the file taken off the phone cannot be attacked without that phone's secure hardware, where the phone has one. Whether the hardware key is bound to an unlocked phone is for a human expert to check (Decision Log #70)."

**9. Sections 1, 13.2 and 13.1: "the findings were fixed" and "fixed before volunteers were invited".**
Decision #70 says invitations started before v0.17 and only the app's link and the consent text are held back until the fixes are checked. So "before volunteers were invited" (13.1, staged-release bullet) is wrong; "before volunteers received the app" is right. The third check also leaves two things for a person outside the code (the branch rule and reviewer of the `pilot-signing` environment, and whether to rotate the signing key), and Decision #70 point 5 lists four points for a human expert. Suggested text for 1 and 13.2: "…the findings that change the code were fixed; four points (the hardware key on an unlocked phone, Argon2 memory on the weakest phones, the face threshold against relatives and photos, and camera-app copies of the selfie) and the protection of the signing key are left for a human expert."

**10. Section 6.8 and 6.10: restore "needs the passport's chip and a matching face", and the backup is returned "to that phone only".**
The service checks only the document proof; the face match is made by the app on the phone, so a modified app can skip it, and the backup goes to whoever proves they hold the document's signed data. Suggested text: "restoring elsewhere still needs the passport's chip and the password; the app also asks for a matching face, but that check runs on the phone only." In 6.10, "returns the encrypted backup to the phone that proves it holds the passport's signed data".

**11. Section 6.8 encrypted backup, "The service cannot open it".**
True for an operator who does not know the person's document data. The operator holds k, so with the document data of a known person (for instance a volunteer it knows) it can compute that person's backup seed and then guess the password through Argon2 (which the text mentions but only as a footnote). The consent says the same ("cannot decrypt it"). Suggested text: "The service cannot open it from what it holds. An operator who also knew a person's document data could compute the backup seed and would then have to guess the password through Argon2, so the password's strength is what protects the backup."

**12. Section 6.10 table and 17.24: in the pilot no revocation path exists.**
The pilot bullet names renewal, recovery ballots and re-enrolment as not built, but item 24 and the table row "the old phone may be in someone else's hands" speak of revocation after a cooling-off period as if it applied. State it once in the pilot bullet: "In the pilot there is no revocation: the identity of a stolen phone stays valid, protected only by the password and the face check." Add the same short sentence to 17.24.

**13. Section 17.27: the small anonymity set of the pilot is not stated.**
About 100 known volunteers on one test network with one operator is a tiny anonymity set; participation statistics and the fact of participation are far easier to link than in the design. Suggested text, appended to 17.27: "With about 100 members known to the organiser, the anonymity set is small, so the fact that someone took part is easier to infer than the design intends."

**14. Sections 9.5 and 15: no mention that the pilot publishes no breakdowns.**
9.5 says breakdowns by verified attributes "can be reported" with a minimum group "for example 50"; Decision #66 and the consent say none are published in the pilot and (for the future) use 10 in the pilot. Suggested text: "In the pilot no breakdowns are published (Decision Log #66, point 3); the consent text will be updated before any are."

**15. Sections 8.11 (Phase 1a paragraph) and 15 row 1a: stale statements about deployed contracts.**
"The deployed contracts are the version with challenges; the reference contracts with the tally proof … will be used from the next deployment" and "Next: … a deployment with the tally proof" contradict section 1, which says the contracts now accept a result only with a proof of correct decryption, and the pilot's deployments paragraph (deployments 4–6). Suggested text: add "(as of 5 November; superseded by deployments 4–6, below)" and remove the item from the Next list or move it to Done.

**16. Section 13.2, languages and names.**
(a) "It is in eight languages" (1) and the translators text should say that the texts are AI-translated and which have been read by a native speaker; Decision #68 point 5 makes that a precondition for volunteers using a language, but the paper does not say whether it has been met. Suggested: "…in eight languages; each text is translated by an AI agent, and a native speaker reads a language's texts before volunteers use it (Decision Log #68)." (b) "each from a different country" holds for the nine agents; among the translators Iran and China repeat (Khayyam/Ferdowsi, Cai Lun/Yan Fu). Say "the nine agents". (c) Privacy: "from him" (13.1, existing since v1.2) discloses the founder's gender; use "from the founder" or "from them". (d) The consent text says the organiser is "the person who invited you" and the project's developers stay anonymous; nothing in the paper names a country, employer, location, or the pilot's country. No leak found.

**17. Section 13.2, link and consent wording.**
(a) The review link `../../reviews/` is broken from `docs/whitepaper/`; it should be `../reviews/`. (b) Sections 1 and 14 say "written consent"; the consent is a tick box in the app after reading the text (consent §12). Suggested: "recorded consent (a tick in the app after reading the consent text)". (c) 6.3 step 1 says the password is chosen at install; in the app it is chosen at enrolment after the selfie (consent §7, step 4). Suggested: "chooses a password at enrolment (6.8)".

## Checked and found consistent

- Password: at least 8 characters, Argon2id 64 MiB, 3 passes; inner layer by password, outer by Keystore key per member; labels and day added visible; five-minute close; wait from the third wrong password (30 s, doubling, up to one hour), kept per member on disk, on the running clock, full wait again after a restart (`Profiles.kt`).
- Backup: key from password and a backup seed from the person's OPRF value; service keeps the ciphertext, once per person, up to 12,000 characters, returns it to a proven later enrolment of the same person; face template and "face" key not in the backup (`Backup.kt`, `Profiles.withoutFace`, `service.js`).
- Face template: 512 numbers, stored in the member's encrypted file, never sent; selfie compared on the phone; three failed selfies cause a ten-minute wait (consent has this; the paper may omit it).
- Member number: derived from k·x under its own tag; shown after enrolment and on refusal; never a sequence.
- No "PIN" or "duress PIN" survives except the deliberate mentions in 6.8, 9.3 and 17.28; no "receipts off by default on shared phones" remains; Greek names appear only in the "Former name" column and in the history of the review process; items 26–28 and the 12.2 shared-phone row agree with 6.8.
- Review of the code: three rounds (v0.16 review, v0.17 re-review, third check) match `docs/reviews/`; the paper correctly says this is not independent review.
- No name, country, employer or location of the founder, and no pilot country, in the text.

## Verdict

v1.3 is a clear improvement: it corrects the headline claim that no real person takes part before review, and its description of the password, the backup and the shared phone is largely accurate to the code. It should not be published as final until findings 1 to 5 are fixed. The first two matter most: the paper tells readers that answers become unreadable after a receipt window (the app has no such window) and that the pilot's enrolment works the way the enclave design does (it does not). Findings 6 to 17 are wording and consistency fixes that can be made in the same pass. After these changes the paper would state the pilot's deviations plainly and would not claim protections the pilot lacks.
