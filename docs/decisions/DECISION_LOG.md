| 43 | **Enrolment uniqueness rules** (2026-10-29; from the second-model review of whitepaper §6). (1) **Document fingerprint from the personal number.** Where a document carries a personal (national) number in its signed data (MRZ optional-data field or DG11), the fingerprint is derived from nationality, that number, date of birth and sex: unique and stable across renewals. Where it does not, the fingerprint uses nationality, name, date of birth and sex, and a match is only a signal: the enclave compares the face with the stored template of that fingerprint, and a clearly different face is a different person. (3) **Revoke before reissue.** After a re-enrolment, the new credential is issued only once the old identity's revocation has been accepted on the ledger. | 47 | **A document key beside the person key** (2026-11-03; founder's proposal). | 59 | **One passport, one member number, one identity on any phone** (2026-11-21; founder's proposal). (1) **Member number.** The phone derives a readable member number, for example `ONE-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX-XXXX` (about 80 bits with a check character), from the value only it obtains in the fingerprint OPRF of the person key (`k·x`), under its own domain tag. It depends only on the person's document data and the service's key, so the same person gets the same number on any phone, after re-enrolment and after renewing the passport; the service, which sees only the fingerprint, cannot compute it from what it stores. It is shown on the phone after enrolment, and when a further enrolment is refused ("this passport is already enrolled as ONE-…; each person enrols once"), so that people without technical knowledge can see the one-person rule at work. It is never sent anywhere, and the app warns against publishing it: a stable number tied to a person must stay private, since whoever holds the service's key and a person's document data could recompute it. (2) **The identity on a new phone.** At enrolment the member chooses a password (at least 8 characters or 6 words). The phone encrypts the identity secret under a key derived, with a slow function such as Argon2, from that password and from the phone-only OPRF value; the service keeps only the ciphertext beside the person key and cannot open it. On a new phone, the member reads the passport and proves it again (a fresh π_doc); when the service finds the person key already enrolled, it returns the ciphertext to that phone only, and the password restores the same identity, with the same number and nothing written to the ledger. Two phones holding one identity give no extra ballots, since both make the same nullifier in every poll. (3) **Limits.** Restoring on a new phone does not disable the old one: for a stolen phone the member uses revocation and re-enrolment (Decision #52) instead. A forgotten password also falls back to that path, giving a new identity and a new number. The face check before each ballot on every phone stays a deterrent only (Decision #54); the nullifier is what prevents a second ballot. Whitepaper §6.8 (the member number was derived from the identity secret) and §6.10 (recovery) are to be revised accordingly in the next version.
