| 30 | **One person, one identity, several verified nationalities.** A member with more than one genuine chip document may add each further document to their existing identity instead of being refused as a duplicate. | 33 | **Face uniqueness at scale** ([spec](../specs/face-uniqueness-at-scale-v0.1.md)). (1) Enrolment first asks whether the person has already enrolled with another document; if so, they are guided to add the document (Decision #30). (2) The main duplicate search compares the new face only with members of the same birth date and sex, with a normal threshold. (3) A second search covers all members with a very strict threshold (false-alarm rate in the order of one in a million), so that an issuer printing different birth dates cannot evade detection. (4) Statistics by document issuer reveal abnormal patterns (Decision #21). (5) The stored face template is refreshed whenever a member adds or renews a document. (6) The threshold favours honest people over catching every duplicate, and error rates are published. (7) A person wrongly flagged as a duplicate has a path to a second review. (8) The accuracy study measures errors by time between photos, for phone selfies, and for 1, 10 and 100 million members. Open question: whether a second biometric is needed if face alone proves insufficient. | Founder (points 1, 2 and 5 proposed by the founder) + team |

| 42 | **Signature suites of the document proof** (2026-10-29; [document-validity proof §5.1](../specs/document-validity-proof-v0.1.md)). (1) One circuit per hash and key size, sharing one template, so that a fix applies to every suite. (2) Where signers use several exponents, the exponent is a private input bound in the signer's leaf, not one circuit per exponent, so the circuit used does not point to one issuer. (3) The leaf names its suite, and a signer is added under a suite only after documents have been seen to use that suite's hash. (4) SHA-1 is supported only because passports in use carry it; the reason it is acceptable here (a second preimage of state-signed data would be needed) is stated in the specification and left to expert review. (5) The next suites are chosen from measured document-signer certificates (trust list §4). | Founder + team |
| 43 | **Enrolment uniqueness rules** (2026-10-29; from the second-model review of whitepaper §6). (1) **Document fingerprint from the personal number.** Where a document carries a personal (national) number in its signed data (MRZ optional-data field or DG11), the fingerprint is derived from nationality, that number, date of birth and sex: unique and stable across renewals. Where it does not, the fingerprint uses nationality, name, date of birth and sex, and a match is only a signal: the enclave compares the face with the stored template of that fingerprint, and a clearly different face is a different person. Which countries carry a stable personal number is surveyed first (schedule 0.16). (2) **Atomic reservation.** The uniqueness service reserves the fingerprint and face template before any credential is issued, so concurrent enrolments with one document cannot both pass. (3) **Revoke before reissue.** After a re-enrolment, the new credential is issued only once the old identity's revocation has been accepted on the ledger. With membership and revocation snapshots fixed when each poll opens, no poll accepts both identities; a state-transition analysis and contract tests are to be published with the ballot specification. | Founder (1, his proposal) + team (2, 3) |
| 44 | **Several poll types, all kept open** (2026-10-30; [cost of large polls](../specs/cost-of-large-polls-v0.1.md)). (1) **Sampled polls**: a verified random sample of members, invited by a private lottery proven in the ballot proof, for estimates such as pre-election polling; cheap enough to be paid by the media and research organisations that commission them. (2) **Open polls**: every eligible member may answer, for questions on which a whole community wants to be heard, including questions no authority has put to a vote; this remains a core purpose of the project. (3) **Ballot rollup with blob data** as the path for very large open polls, after recursive aggregation. (4) The choice of ledger is to be compared again, including non-blockchain public logs (activity 0.17). (5) Grants stay an option; a legal structure will probably be needed for administration and money, and whether its officers can stay anonymous is to be studied with legal advice. | Founder |
