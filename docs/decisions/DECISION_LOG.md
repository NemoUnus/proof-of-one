| 42 | **Signature suites of the document proof** (2026-10-29; [document-validity proof §5.1](../specs/document-validity-proof-v0.1.md)). (1) One circuit per hash and key size, sharing one template, so that a fix applies to every suite. (2) Where signers use several exponents, the exponent is a private input bound in the signer's leaf, not one circuit per exponent, so the circuit used does not point to one issuer. (3) The leaf names its suite, and a signer is added under a suite only after documents have been seen to use that suite's hash. (4) SHA-1 is supported only because passports in use carry it; the reason it is acceptable here (a second preimage of state-signed data would be needed) is stated in the specification and left to expert review. (5) The next suites are chosen from measured document-signer certificates (trust list §4). | Founder + team |
| 43 | **Enrolment uniqueness rules** (2026-10-29; from the second-model review of whitepaper §6). (1) **Document fingerprint from the personal number.** Where a document carries a personal (national) number in its signed data (MRZ optional-data field or DG11), the fingerprint is derived from nationality, that number, date of birth and sex: unique and stable across renewals. Where it does not, the fingerprint uses nationality, name, date of birth and sex, and a match is only a signal: the enclave compares the face with the stored template of that fingerprint, and a clearly different face is a different person. Which countries carry a stable personal number is surveyed first (schedule 0.16). (2) **Atomic reservation.** The uniqueness service reserves the fingerprint and face template before any credential is issued, so concurrent enrolments with one document cannot both pass. (3) **Revoke before reissue.** After a re-enrolment, the new credential is issued only once the old identity's revocation has been accepted on the ledger. With membership and revocation snapshots fixed when each poll opens, no poll accepts both identities; a state-transition analysis and contract tests are to be published with the ballot specification. | Founder (1, his proposal) + team (2, 3) |

| 45 | **Checking one's own answer comes first** (2026-11-03). Opinion polls, not binding elections, are the first use. | 47 | **A document key beside the person key** (2026-11-03; founder's proposal). Each enrolled document gets a **document key** derived from its issuing state and document number, valid until the document expires. The **person key** (the document fingerprint of 6.6: national number where confirmed, otherwise name, birth date and sex) stays the basis of the identity, and the face check stays tied to it. On a new enrolment: (1) a known document key means the same document again, refused at once without a face comparison; (2) a known person key with a new document key from the same issuer, and a matching face, means a renewal: the document key is replaced; (3) the same from another issuer means another nationality: a document key is added (Decision #30). The system works this out itself and asks the member only to confirm. The app reminds the member before the document expires. Both keys are computed with the keyed OPRF and kept only inside the uniqueness service; neither is ever written to the ledger, because document numbers come from a small space and a plain hash could be reversed. | Founder + team |
| 48 | **Re-votes in log polls are ordered by revision number** (2026-11-04; [transparency-log ballots](../specs/transparency-log-ballots-v0.1.md) §4). In polls recorded in transparency logs, each new ballot of a member carries a higher revision number, bound by the ballot proof and capped by the poll; the ballot with the highest number counts, ties going to the smaller ballot hash. Ordering by time was rejected because a log controls when it asks witnesses to cosign, and so could choose which of a member's answers counts. Accepted trade-off: someone holding a member's phone can force the highest revision, after which the member cannot replace that ballot. This follows Decision #45 and the known limit on coercion with the phone in hand (whitepaper §17). | Founder + team |

| 52 | **Participation stays anonymous; key backup is encouraged** (2026-11-08; from the second-model review of whitepaper v1.0, finding 6). Making public which members took part, with only the answers encrypted, was considered and rejected: whether someone took part is itself sensitive where the project is most needed; it would leave one layer of protection instead of two, so that a future break of the tally key or of today's cryptography would link every answer to a member code on a permanent public record; and it would show who changed their answer. The member alone can see and check their own recorded ballot (#45). Instead: (1) a revoked identity's ballots are refused in polls that are still open from the moment of revocation; (2) a member who restores their old secret from a backup at recovery can have that identity's ballots in open polls removed from the encrypted totals and answer again, which a thief cannot do without passing the face check; (3) the app encourages every member to make a key backup at enrolment and says what it protects. Point 2 is recorded as a design and implemented after the current contracts. | Founder + team |
| 53 | **Fingerprint key and result checks** (2026-11-08; from the second-model review of whitepaper v1.0, findings 1–3). (1) **Split OPRF key.** The key of the fingerprint OPRF is shared among several independent operators (for example 2 of 3), so that no single operator can evaluate guesses of name, birth date and sex; until then the key is used only inside attested enclave code, with rate limits and a public count of evaluations. The whitepaper states plainly that the OPRF protects against outsiders and a stolen database, not against whoever holds the key. (2) **No scheduled key rotation.** Fingerprints must stay the same for years so that a returning person is recognised, and they cannot be recomputed without the inputs, which are not kept. The key is replaced only if it is suspected to be compromised: old and new keys then run side by side, each member moves to the new key the next time they prove their document, and the old key is destroyed at the end. (3) **Results checked at publication.** The contract verifies every trustee's decryption proof when the result is published, instead of waiting for a challenge, so that a wrong result can never become final because nobody challenged it. This costs about 2 million gas once per poll. *Amended 9 November 2026, with the founder's agreement:* that estimate was for one trustee proof; checking all of them directly would need about 26 million gas, above the limit per transaction, so the contract checks a tally proof π_tally instead, about 853,000 gas whatever the number of trustees (implementation note N32). | Founder + team |
| 54 | **Several members on one phone** (2026-11-09; founder's proposal). So that people without a suitable smartphone or the skills to use one, such as elderly parents, or people in places where few phones are available, can take part: (1) one phone may hold up to **4 profiles**, each a separate member with their own identity; (2) each profile opens with its own PIN, and its identity secret is encrypted separately, so that another user of the phone cannot open it; (3) on a phone with more than one profile the receipt window defaults to none, so that people sharing a phone cannot see each other's answers; (4) the app checks the member's face against their enrolment before each ballot, as a **deterrent only**: a local check can be bypassed by a modified app, and it shows that the member was present, not that the choice was theirs. A face check by the enclave at each ballot was rejected, because it would tell the service who is voting and when (Decision Log #52); (5) enrolment always needs the member's own live face; (6) the number of phones with several profiles is published in aggregate, so that mass enrolment on a few devices is visible. Accepted risk: someone who shares a phone with a member may answer for them, as with assisted or postal voting; acceptable while polls are not binding (Decision Log #9), and to be reviewed before any binding use. Research: a member's key on their own NFC card, used on a shared phone. | Founder + team |
